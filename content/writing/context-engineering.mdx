Prompt engineering optimized a single call. Context engineering optimizes the **whole session** — what enters the window, in what order, for how long, and at what cost.

## Seven patterns I actually ship

**Window selection.** Route to sub-agents with task-specific payloads, not full codebase dumps.

**Compression.** A 4KB tool response becomes a 200-token structured summary before re-injection.

**RAG integration.** Hybrid retrieval (BM25 + dense + rerank) per step — not one naive top-k at session start.

**Shared context layers.** The Planner writes a spec; the Coder reads it — not the Planner's chain-of-thought.

**Routing.** Simple tasks to SLMs, hard reasoning to frontier models. Context routing is how you get there without quality loss.

**Eviction.** Drop consumed tool results. A filesystem listing from step 2 should not occupy tokens in step 8.

**Caching.** Embeddings, retrieval results, compiled knowledge — if the corpus has not changed, do not re-retrieve.

## What the numbers look like

In production agent workflows I have built, applying all seven typically reduces token consumption **30–50%** per session. That is not from smaller models alone — it is from sending less junk to whatever model you use.

If you are still tuning system prompts while agents burn 100K tokens per task, you are optimizing the wrong layer.
