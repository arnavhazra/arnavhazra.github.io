## The category error

Most AI products in 2026 are content generators wearing enterprise costumes. Chatbots that summarize. Copilots that draft. RAG systems that retrieve and paraphrase. They optimize for **text output** — and measure success in tokens generated, not decisions made.

This is a category error. The highest-value AI products won't generate content — they'll **structure decisions**.

## What decision infrastructure means

A decision infrastructure platform takes raw catalog data — products, properties, specifications — and transforms it into:

- **Constraint-based filtering** (budget, safety rating, location)
- **Multi-axis comparison** (price vs. features vs. trade-offs)
- **Explainable ranking** (why option A beats option B for *your* criteria)

The buyer doesn't read a paragraph about cars. They interact with a decision matrix that respects their constraints and surfaces trade-offs they hadn't considered.

## Why now

Three forces converge:

1. **Catalog explosion** — e-commerce and B2B catalogs are too large for human comparison
2. **Decision paralysis** — $412B in automotive alone lost to buyers who can't choose
23× more decision points in high-consideration purchases vs. simple buys
3. **LLM maturity** — models can now structure and explain, not just generate prose

The opportunity isn't "add AI to your website copy." It's "replace the comparison page with a decision engine."

Automotive: safety rating × price × budget tier.

Electronics: spec comparison with constraint filtering.

SOC2-compliant. Enterprise-ready. Not a demo — a deployment model.

## For founders and investors

The next wave of AI startups won't compete on model access — that's commoditized. They'll compete on **decision architecture**: how well you turn unstructured catalogs into structured choices that convert.

Content generation is a feature. Decision infrastructure is a category.

LLMs write text. We build decisions.
